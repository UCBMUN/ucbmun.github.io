About the Committee

In the wake of France's final series of nuclear tests at Mururoa and Fangataufa Atolls in late 1995, the tranquil waters of French Polynesia have become the epicenter of an international political storm. What Paris defended as an essential step toward ensuring the reliability of its nuclear deterrent has instead ignited fierce condemnation across the Pacific and beyond. Environmental organizations warn of irreversible damage to fragile marine ecosystems, neighboring island nations demand accountability for radioactive contamination, and mass protests sweep through Tahiti as local communities question whether their homeland has been sacrificed in the name of national security.

The controversy extends far beyond the atolls themselves. Diplomatic relations between France and its Pacific neighbors have deteriorated as governments weigh sanctions, boycotts, and coordinated international action. Activists, scientists, and indigenous leaders call for transparency regarding the long-term environmental and public health consequences of decades of nuclear testing, while French officials struggle to maintain control of the narrative amid mounting global scrutiny. The balance between strategic military interests, environmental stewardship, and colonial governance has never been more precarious.

As delegates convene, the tests have ended, but the crisis has only begun. Reports of ecological degradation, fears of radioactive leakage, and demands for compensation and political autonomy dominate the agenda. The international community looks to France for answers, while the people of French Polynesia seek justice, recognition, and a voice in determining their future. Every decision made in this committee will shape not only the legacy of France's nuclear program but also the future of the Pacific region itself.

Welcome to the aftermath of the 1995 French Polynesian nuclear tests. Will you restore trust through diplomacy, scientific transparency, and environmental action, or will competing interests deepen divisions and leave the scars of nuclear testing to endure for generations? The fate of French Polynesia, and France's place on the world stage, now rests in your hands.
