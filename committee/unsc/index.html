About the Committee

The sands of the Sinai are shifting, and the ""artery of empire"" has been severed. In a defiant roar from Alexandria, President Gamal Abdel Nasser has nationalized the Suez Canal, reclaiming for Egypt the rightful property of its people. For decades, this narrow strip of water has been the vital link between West and East and a symbol of European influence in the Middle East. Now, that link has been seized with the stroke of a pen, leaving the world reeling in disbelief. In London and Paris, the atmosphere is one of outrage and desperation. To the British, the canal is the lifeline of their dwindling empire and a crucial conduit for oil, a necessary resource in a rapidly-modernizing world. To the French, Nasser’s defiance fans the flames of rebellion already spreading across North Africa. The colonial powers are at a crossroads: do they accept a new world order where sovereignty belongs to the post-colonial state, or do they strike back to preserve the remnants of their global dominance? The world watches as the shadow of the Cold War looms over Egypt. Washington counsels restraint, fearful that war will drive the Arab world into the waiting arms of the Soviet Union, while Moscow senses an opportunity to dismantle Western influence. The United Nations serves as a potential arbiter of a peaceful resolution, but calls for cooperation are met with Cairo’s unwavering demand for total independence. The clock is ticking, and the fragile peace of the post-war era nears its breaking point. As delegates in this emergency session of the UNSC, you face a world in transition. Will you champion liberation and the right of a sovereign state to control its resources? Or will you defend the sanctity of international treaties and the stability of global commerce? In this committee, you will navigate a labyrinth of secret alliances, economic pressures, and mounting military threats. In the meantime, the currents of history pull you toward an uncertain horizon. Welcome to the Suez Canal Crisis, 1956.
